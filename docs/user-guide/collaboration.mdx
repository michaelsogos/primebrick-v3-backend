# Working together on the same record

Picture this: you are editing a customer's address while, at the very same
moment, a colleague (Mr Brown) in another office updates that customer's phone number.
In most software this ends badly — whoever saves *last* silently overwrites
whoever saved *first*, and nobody even notices the lost change until a
delivery goes to the wrong address. Primebrick is built so that this simply
cannot happen.

## What you see while someone else is there

Open a record that a colleague is already viewing or editing and you will
know it: the interface shows you **who else is on the record** and, as they
work, **which field they are focused on**. You are never typing blind into
a shared document — presence is always visible.

## What happens when you both save

This is where the protection actually kicks in. When Mr Brown saves
while you still have the record open, you are told immediately — and what
happens next depends on *what* they changed:

| Scenario | What Primebrick does | What you see |
|---|---|---|
| They changed a **different field** than yours | The change is merged in automatically — nothing is lost, nothing to decide | A notification that the record was updated; the new value appears |
| They changed the **same field** you are editing | A genuine conflict — both versions are kept and presented side by side | A comparison of *their value* vs *your value*, and you choose which survives |

The rule is simple: **overlapping edits are never auto-resolved for you** —
a human always decides a real conflict — but **non-overlapping edits never
need your attention at all**.

## When does this apply?

Everywhere it matters. The protection is automatic on any record type that
keeps a change history — customers, users, organisations, roles, and every
other auditable entity. You do not enable it, configure it, or opt in: it
is part of how records work.

## Why it exists

- **It removes a whole class of silent data loss.** "Last save wins" is the
  default behaviour of most software and the root cause of an enormous
  amount of real-world errors.
- **It makes simultaneous work safe.** Two people no longer need to
  coordinate ("are you done in that customer yet?") before opening the
  same record.
- **It keeps a record of who did what.** Because every change is tracked,
  the merge always knows exactly which fields each person touched — that
  same history powers the *Audit history* action you find on every record.

## Do you need to do anything?

No. There is nothing to switch on and nothing to learn beyond two habits:

1. **Glance at the presence indicators** when you open a busy record —
  you will immediately know whether someone else is in there with you.
2. **When a conflict appears, read it before choosing.** The dialog shows
  you both values precisely so you can make an informed decision instead
  of guessing.
